FAQ

Common questions.

No. Under Arizona law (ARS 20-263), a standalone comprehensive glass claim can't be used to raise your premium.

Most Arizona comprehensive policies waive the deductible for glass repair entirely, and many waive it for replacement too. We verify your specific policy before any work starts.

No. Arizona law (ARS 20-469) protects your right to choose your own repair shop, regardless of who your insurer suggests.

Small chips and short cracks outside the driver's direct line of sight are usually repairable. Long cracks, damage at the edge, or anything obstructing the driver's view typically calls for a full replacement. We'll tell you which applies before we start.

Many vehicles from roughly 2018 onward have a forward-facing camera mounted to the windshield for lane-keep assist and automatic braking. Replacing the glass moves that camera, so it needs to be recalibrated — we do this on-site as part of the job.

Chip repairs run about 20–30 minutes. Full replacements typically take 45–75 minutes, plus safe-drive-away cure time your technician will confirm on site.
