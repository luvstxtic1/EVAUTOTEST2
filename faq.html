No. Under Arizona law (ARS 20-263), a standalone comprehensive glass claim can't be used to raise your premium. This is different from an at-fault collision claim, which can affect your rate.

Most Arizona comprehensive policies waive the deductible for glass repair entirely, and many waive it for replacement too. We verify your specific policy before any work starts, so you'll know your exact cost — often $0 — up front.

No. Arizona law (ARS 20-469) protects your right to choose your own repair shop, regardless of who your insurer suggests. Their "preferred" list is a convenience for them, not a requirement for you.

Small chips and short cracks outside the driver's direct line of sight are usually repairable with resin injection. Long cracks, damage at the edge of the glass, or anything obstructing the driver's view typically calls for a full replacement. We'll confirm which applies to your situation before we start any work.

Many vehicles from roughly 2018 onward have a forward-facing camera mounted to the windshield that supports lane-keep assist, automatic emergency braking, and adaptive cruise control. Replacing the glass moves that camera slightly, so it needs to be recalibrated to work correctly again. We carry the equipment to do this on-site as part of the replacement.

Chip repairs typically run 20–30 minutes. Full replacements usually take 45–75 minutes, plus safe-drive-away cure time — your technician will tell you exactly how long to wait before driving.

Every repair and replacement is covered against leaks, wind noise, and installation defects for as long as you own the vehicle. If something's off, we come back and fix it at no charge.
